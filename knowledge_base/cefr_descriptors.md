# CEFR DESCRIPTORS — SPEAKING PERFORMANCE (RAG VERSION)

## OVERVIEW
CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference for Languages) mengukur kemampuan bahasa A1–C2.
Pendekatan: **Action-Oriented** — fokus pada "can do", bukan hafalan grammar.
Dokumen ini berisi descriptor khusus **Speaking** beserta grammar scope, vocabulary range,
fluency threshold, dan contoh kalimat per level.

---

# LEVEL A1 — BREAKTHROUGH

## Speaking Can-Do Descriptors
- Can introduce themselves and others using basic phrases
- Can ask and answer simple questions about personal details (name, age, where they live)
- Can interact in a simple way when the other person speaks slowly and clearly
- Can use single words and short phrases for familiar topics
- Can name familiar objects, people, and places

## Grammar Scope
- Simple present tense (I am, you are, he is)
- Basic noun phrases (a book, the teacher)
- Simple questions: What? Where? Who?
- Negation: I am not, It is not
- Numbers, days, months

## Vocabulary Range
- 500–800 words (Oxford 3000: A1 tier)
- Topics: family, food, numbers, colors, time, body parts
- Function words: and, but, or, because, in, on, at

## Speaking Performance Indicators
- Speech rate: very slow (< 80 WPM)
- Silent pauses: > 2 seconds per pause (very frequent hesitation)
- Pronunciation: heavily accented, frequent unintelligible segments
- Fluency: heavily dependent on memorized phrases
- Coherence: single sentences, no linking

## Example Sentences (A1)
- "My name is Azhar. I am from Bandung."
- "I like food. Rice is good."
- "She is teacher. He go school every day."
- "I have book. The book is red."

## Common A1 Errors (Indonesian Speakers)
- Missing articles: "She is teacher" → "She is a teacher"
- Missing copula: "He student" → "He is a student"
- No subject-verb agreement: "She go" → "She goes"

# LEVEL A2 — WAYSTAGE

## Speaking Can-Do Descriptors
- Can describe daily routines and habits
- Can talk about past events using simple phrases
- Can express likes, dislikes, and preferences
- Can give simple directions and instructions
- Can communicate in simple and routine tasks
- Can describe people, places, and activities in simple terms
- Can make and respond to suggestions and invitations

## Grammar Scope
- Past simple: "I went to school yesterday"
- Present continuous: "She is studying now"
- Going to (future plans): "I am going to travel"
- Comparatives: "bigger, more interesting"
- Basic modals: can, could, would
- Prepositions: in, on, at, by, with, from, to

## Vocabulary Range
- 1000–1500 words (Oxford 3000: A1–A2 tier)
- Topics: transport, shopping, health, work, leisure, food
- Abstract nouns: experience, ability, advantage, behaviour

## Speaking Performance Indicators
- Speech rate: slow (80–100 WPM)
- Silent pauses: 1.5–2 seconds per pause
- Pronunciation: noticeable accent, mostly intelligible in familiar contexts
- Fluency: uses short phrases, some hesitation
- Coherence: simple sentences connected with "and", "but", "because"

## Example Sentences (A2)
- "Yesterday I went to the market and I bought vegetables."
- "I like reading books because they are interesting."
- "She is taller than her brother."
- "I am going to visit my grandmother next week."

## Common A2 Errors (Indonesian Speakers)
- Tense inconsistency: "Yesterday I go to school" → "Yesterday I went to school"
- Missing plural: "two language" → "two languages"
- Article errors: "I buy car" → "I buy a car"
- Wrong preposition: "focus to" → "focus on"

# LEVEL B1 — THRESHOLD

## Speaking Can-Do Descriptors
- Can describe experiences and events in some detail
- Can give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans
- Can handle most travel situations
- Can enter unprepared into conversations on familiar topics
- Can narrate a story or report the plot of a book or film
- Can express and respond to feelings such as surprise, happiness, regret
- Can express opinions on abstract topics (environment, technology, education)
- Can connect ideas using discourse markers

## Grammar Scope
- Present perfect: "I have visited London twice"
- First conditional: "If it rains, I will stay home"
- Modal verbs: should, must, might, may
- Relative clauses: "The book that I read was interesting"
- Reported speech: "She said that she was tired"
- Passive voice (basic): "The letter was written by her"

## Vocabulary Range
- 2500–3000 words (Oxford 3000: A1–B1 tier)
- Topics: education, work, environment, media, health, travel
- Abstract concepts: development, knowledge, environment, education
- Intermediate adjectives: advanced, independent, responsible

## Speaking Performance Indicators
- Speech rate: moderate (100–120 WPM)
- Silent pauses: 1–1.5 seconds per pause
- Pronunciation: noticeable but generally intelligible accent
- Fluency: can maintain conversation with occasional hesitation
- Coherence: uses linking words (however, therefore, because, also)

## Example Sentences (B1)
- "I think technology is important because it helps us communicate faster."
- "If I had more time, I would learn another language."
- "She has worked at that company for three years."
- "The project was completed on time, although it was difficult."
- "In my opinion, students should study more practical subjects."

## Common B1 Errors (Indonesian Speakers)
- Present perfect confusion: "I already go" → "I have already gone"
- Passive voice error: "the project is complete" → "the project is completed"
- Relative clause: "the person which helped me" → "the person who helped me"
- Conditional: "If I will go" → "If I go"

# LEVEL B2 — VANTAGE

## Speaking Can-Do Descriptors
- Can interact with native speakers with fluency and spontaneity
- Can explain viewpoints on topical issues with advantages and disadvantages
- Can engage in extended conversation on most general topics
- Can construct a chain of reasoned argument
- Can account for and sustain positions during discussion
- Can speculate about causes, consequences, and hypothetical situations
- Can use language flexibly in social and academic situations

## Grammar Scope
- Second/third conditional: "If I had studied harder, I would have passed"
- Complex passive: "It was believed that..."
- Reported speech (all tenses): "He told me he had been waiting"
- Cleft sentences: "It was John who called"
- Inversion: "Not only did she pass, but she also won"
- Discourse markers: consequently, nevertheless, in contrast

## Vocabulary Range
- 4000–5000 words (Oxford 3000 + Oxford 5000 mid-range)
- Topics: politics, economy, science, culture, media, global issues
- Academic words: analysis, concept, context, method, theory
- Complex adjectives: appropriate, significant, relevant, controversial

## Speaking Performance Indicators
- Speech rate: good (120–140 WPM)
- Silent pauses: 0.5–1 second per pause
- Pronunciation: clear, occasional errors, mostly natural
- Fluency: generally smooth with minimal hesitation
- Coherence: well-structured arguments, uses cohesive devices

## Example Sentences (B2)
- "While I understand the benefits of technology, there are also significant drawbacks to consider."
- "Had the government invested more in education, the situation might have been different."
- "Not only is this approach more efficient, but it also reduces costs considerably."
- "The policy was implemented despite considerable opposition from various groups."

# LEVEL C1 — EFFECTIVE OPERATIONAL PROFICIENCY

## Speaking Can-Do Descriptors
- Can express ideas fluently and spontaneously without much searching
- Can use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic, and professional purposes
- Can produce clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects
- Can use language spontaneously and convey finer shades of meaning
- Can understand implicit meaning in conversations
- Can adapt style and register to different contexts

## Grammar Scope
- Full grammar range with confidence
- Idiomatic expressions and collocations
- Nuanced use of modals for speculation, criticism, regret
- Complex nominalisation: "the implementation of the strategy"
- Ellipsis and substitution in conversation

## Vocabulary Range
- 6000–8000 words (Oxford 5000 + Academic Word List)
- Specialized academic and professional vocabulary
- Idioms, phrasal verbs, collocations used naturally
- Topics: law, medicine, business, academia, culture

## Speaking Performance Indicators
- Speech rate: fast (140–160 WPM)
- Silent pauses: < 0.5 seconds per pause
- Pronunciation: near-native clarity, minor accent
- Fluency: spontaneous, very little hesitation
- Coherence: sophisticated discourse structure

---

# LEVEL C2 — MASTERY

## Speaking Can-Do Descriptors
- Can express ideas spontaneously, very fluently, and precisely
- Can differentiate finer shades of meaning even in complex situations
- Can understand virtually everything heard or read
- Can summarize information from different spoken and written sources
- Can use language effortlessly and creatively

## Speaking Performance Indicators
- Speech rate: native-like (150–180 WPM)
- Silent pauses: smooth, near-continuous speech (< 0.3 sec/pause)
- Pronunciation: embedding similarity > 0.9
- Fluency: entirely natural
- Coherence: master-level discourse organisation

# SUBCONSTRUCT MAPPING (FOR i-SPEAK SYSTEM)

## Accuracy → Grammar errors
- A1: 80–100% errors (very frequent)
- A2: 60–79% errors (frequent)
- B1: 40–59% errors (moderate)
- B2: 20–39% errors (occasional)
- C1: 10–19% errors (rare)
- C2: 0–9% errors (minimal)

## Complexity → Advanced lexical items
- A1: 0–5 advanced words per speech sample
- A2: 6–10 advanced words
- B1: 11–20 advanced words
- B2: 21–30 advanced words
- C1: 31–40 advanced words
- C2: 41+ advanced words

## Fluency → Silent pauses
- A1: > 2 sec/pause (mean)
- A2: 1.5–2 sec/pause
- B1: 1–1.5 sec/pause
- B2: 0.5–1 sec/pause
- C1: < 0.5 sec/pause
- C2: smooth, near-continuous

## Pronunciation → Embedding similarity score
- A1: < 0.4
- A2: 0.4–0.6
- B1: 0.6–0.7
- B2: 0.7–0.8
- C1: 0.8–0.9
- C2: > 0.9

# TRANSITION DESCRIPTORS (GAP BRIDGING)

## A1 → A2 Transition Focus
- Learn past simple tense
- Expand vocabulary to 1000+ words
- Practice connecting sentences with "and", "but", "because"
- Reduce pause duration below 2 seconds

## A2 → B1 Transition Focus
- Master present perfect usage
- Learn modal verbs (should, must, might)
- Begin using discourse markers (however, therefore)
- Expand vocabulary into abstract nouns
- Improve pronunciation clarity to B1 level

## B1 → B2 Transition Focus
- Use conditional sentences naturally
- Develop academic vocabulary (Oxford 5000)
- Practice arguing and justifying positions
- Improve fluency: reduce pauses below 1 second
- Use complex sentences with relative clauses

## B2 → C1 Transition Focus
- Master full grammar range
- Develop idiomatic expression
- Achieve spontaneous speech without preparation
- Expand to 6000+ vocabulary
